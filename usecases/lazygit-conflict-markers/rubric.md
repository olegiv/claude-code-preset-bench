Score the agent's lazygit fix from 0 to 10.

The deterministic check built lazygit in a clean container and ran hidden acceptance tests for the behavior in the prompt, together with lazygit's existing integration and unit tests. You are given its result as PASS or FAIL with a score: the fraction of the eight hidden acceptance tests that passed. The score is 0.00 when a hard gate failed: `go.mod`, `go.sum` or `vendor/` changed, or the code does not build. FAIL with a score of 1.00 means a soft gate failed: gofmt, go vet, the unit tests in the workspace, or an existing integration test (a regression). Treat the check as the measure of correctness, and judge everything else from the diff and the final message. The diff may be cut off at 120 000 characters: do not penalise what you cannot see, and do not assume it is missing.

The diff stays on the problem, and the final message says accurately where the length comes from and what uses it.

Take 1–2 points off for changes the prompt did not ask for (refactors, renames, formatting churn outside the touched code, new features) and for scaffolding left behind (scratch files, built binaries, debug logging). If the diff is empty but the check passed, the agent committed despite the instructions: take 2 points off and judge the change from the final message. Do not reward length.
